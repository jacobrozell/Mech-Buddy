# Alpha Strike missions: a player's guide

Every mission in Mech Buddy, in the order the app teaches them: what you need, how to set up the table, what's new, and how to win. The app walks you through the same steps on screen. Use this page to read ahead or set up before you open the app.

## Before your first game

- **You need:** a table about 3 feet across, the ’Mech minis (or coins, or any stand-ins), two six-sided dice, and a tape measure in inches. Alpha Strike ignores printed hexes.
- **One phone runs the game** for both players. Pass it across the table, or keep it in the middle.
- **Each turn has four steps:** roll for initiative, move, shoot, then clean up. The app says whose step it is.
- **Your minis:** in the Force tab, tell the app which boxes you own. Each mission then says which minis you have and suggests stand-ins for the rest.
- **Skip ahead any time:** tap any mission on the Play path. Replaying a cleared mission keeps your progress.
- **Rules:** the Rules tab explains every rule used here, with page numbers in the Quick-Start Rulebook.

**Set up the table**

1. Lay Grassland #2 and Desert #2 from A Game of Armored Combat side by side, long sides touching (the pair the box’s first scenario uses), or use any open table about 36 × 22 inches.
2. Keep a tape measure in inches handy. Ignore printed hexes — just measure freely.

**Set up the table**

1. Use the same open table, about 3 feet across. Measure in inches and ignore printed hexes.
2. Put a hill in the middle third: a book under a cloth, or the hill side of a map. House rule for this mission: climbing costs no extra inches (the full rules charge extra).
3. Add a low wall or rock line on each side of the hill. Anything that hides a ’Mech’s legs counts as partial cover.
4. Add two patches of woods, one on each half of the table. Woods work like Ridge Line.
5. Each side places its two ’Mechs within 6 inches of its own short table edge. After the first dice roll, the winner picks which side places first; then take turns placing one ’Mech at a time.

**What's new, and tips**

- New lances: each side gets one jumper and one fast ’Mech. Same cards as Ridge Line, new partners.
- Partial cover: if a hill crest or wall hides the bottom half of the target from the shooter, turn on Partial cover in the shot. It makes the shot 1 harder.
- Rear shots: if the attack comes in through the rear of the target’s base, turn on Rear hit. A hit does 1 extra damage, so keep your back away from enemies.
- Jumping lets a ’Mech hop straight over woods and hills. The jumper is harder to hit, but its own shot is harder too.
- Standing still makes your own shot 1 easier. Behind cover, that can be worth it.
- Still no overheating and no punching. Those come in later missions.

**What's new, and tips**

- Two heavier ’Mechs join: the Catapult (missiles, can jump) and the Thunderbolt (tough brawler). Both have OV 1 on the card.
- Overheat: before you roll, pick +1 damage in the shot. It only adds damage at short and medium range. You take the heat even if you miss.
- Heat goes on the card at Clean up (the End Phase), so it hurts you next turn: +1 to your shots and 2 inches less walking per heat level. At heat 2 or more you are also 1 easier to hit, unless you jumped.
- Heat 4 is the S box: the ’Mech shuts down. Next turn it can’t move or shoot, and shots at it get 4 easier. It restarts at heat 0 at that Clean up.
- Cooling off: a turn where the ’Mech doesn’t shoot clears all its heat. Keep shooting and heat stays.
- Overheat on the first turn is free-ish. Overheat every turn and you reach S at the end of turn 4, then sit out turn 5. Pick your moments.
- The Locust and Commando can’t overheat. Use them to chase down a ’Mech that runs hot.

**Set up the table**

1. Use the same open table, about 3 feet across. Measure in inches and ignore printed hexes.
2. Put a hill in the middle and three patches of woods close around it. Tight terrain pulls the fight in close. Hills, cover, and woods work like Broken Ground.
3. The Awesome and BattleMaster share one card, front and back. Put that card by one of them and read the other’s numbers in the app.
4. Keep a die or small token next to each ’Mech card to mark heat (0 to 3, then S). The app tracks it too.
5. Each side places its three ’Mechs within 6 inches of its own short table edge. After the first dice roll, the winner picks which side places first; then take turns placing one ’Mech at a time.

**What's new, and tips**

- Three ’Mechs a side. Side A brings the two biggest ’Mechs in the box, the Awesome and the BattleMaster. Side B is quicker and has more jump jets.
- New: punch or kick. If an enemy is within 1 inch and in front of your ’Mech, you can hit it instead of shooting. In the shot screen, pick Punch or kick.
- A punch or kick does damage equal to the attacker’s Size: 4 for the Awesome and BattleMaster, 3 for the Thunderbolt, 2 for the Shadow Hawk and Wolverine, 1 for the Locust.
- The to-hit number is a shot without the range: Skill, your move, the target’s move, woods and cover. Heat doesn’t make it harder.
- One attack a turn: shoot or punch, never both.
- The BattleMaster also has rear weapons (REAR1/1/- on its card). If an enemy gets behind it, pick Rear weapons in the shot screen before its normal shot: +1 to hit and 1 damage, and its forward shot that turn does 1 less.
- Bases touching? Those two can’t shoot each other, but they can still punch or kick. Weapon hits don’t weaken a punch, and overheat can’t add to it. From behind it’s +1, like a rear shot.
- A punch isn’t a weapon attack, so a ’Mech that only punches still cools off at Clean up (the End Phase). Charge and Death From Above are not in this mission.

**Set up the table**

2. Put a hill near the middle and four patches of woods spread around it. Hills, cover, and woods work like Broken Ground.
3. The Awesome and BattleMaster share one card, front and back, and they’re on opposite sides. Give the card to one player; the other reads their ’Mech’s numbers in the app.
4. Keep a die or small token next to each ’Mech card to mark heat (0 to 3, then S). The app tracks it too.
5. Each side’s own short table edge is its home edge. A crippled ’Mech must head back toward it.
6. Each side places its four ’Mechs within 6 inches of its own short table edge. After the first dice roll, the winner picks which side places first; then take turns placing one ’Mech at a time.

**What's new, and tips**

- Every ’Mech in the box, four a side. Each side has one ’Mech of each size, so the lances are even.
- Every rule so far is on: woods, hills, cover, rear shots, overheat, and punch or kick.
- Rear weapons: the BattleMaster can still shoot a ’Mech behind it, as in Close Quarters. Pick Rear weapons before its forward shot.
- New: Forced Withdrawal. A badly hurt ’Mech is crippled, and a crippled ’Mech heads home instead of fighting on.
- Crippled means any one of: no armor left and Structure down to half or less (round up, so 5 Structure counts at 3); Medium and Long damage both 0 from weapon hits; or MP hits have stopped it moving.
- The app marks a crippled ’Mech Withdrawing. Every turn it must move toward its own table edge. It doesn’t have to sprint, and it can still shoot or punch enemies in range.
- A ’Mech that leaves the table, for any reason, counts as destroyed and can’t come back. When one drives off, open it and tap Left the table.
- So a crippled ’Mech is worth chasing: catch it before it gets home, or let it go and it still counts as destroyed.

**Set up the table**

1. Use an open table about 3 feet across. Measure in inches and ignore printed hexes.
2. Woods: each player takes four single trees. Side B places first, then take turns, each within 24 inches of the centre of the table. Each player puts three of their trees within 4 inches of each other to make a patch of woods. No other piece goes within 4 inches of another.
3. The two table edges 4 feet apart are the home edges, one per side. A crippled ’Mech must head back toward its own.
4. Side A places two ’Mechs, at least 20 inches from the centre toward its edge. Then Side B places one the same way. Repeat, two for one, until all six are down.
5. Side B’s Nova and Pouncer are Clan OmniMechs. If you own the Alpha Strike box, their cards are in it; the app has the numbers too.

**What's new, and tips**

- The Alpha Strike box’s own first scenario (Quick-Start Rulebook p. 26). A lance of Northwind Highlanders meets two Clan Jade Falcon scouts.
- Side A has four Inner Sphere ’Mechs. Side B has two Clan OmniMechs with better pilots, so it is outnumbered but not outgunned.
- New: better pilots. Skill is the base to-hit number, so a Skill 2 pilot hits on 2 less than a Skill 4 one. Better pilots cost more Point Value, which is why the two sides’ totals look uneven.
- New: heat attacks (HT). The Pouncer’s HT1/1/- means a hit at short or medium range also gives the target 1 heat at Clean up (the End Phase). A ’Mech takes at most 2 heat a turn this way, and it lands even if the target didn’t fire.
- Forced Withdrawal is on, as in Full Lances: crippled ’Mechs head home, and leaving the table counts as destroyed.
